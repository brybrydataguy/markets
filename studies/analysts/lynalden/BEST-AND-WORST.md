# Best and worst takes, and recurring points

As assessed on November 7, 2026, the strongest recurring skill is identifying structural pressures and patient investment opportunities. The weaker pattern is translating those pressures into near-term policy limits, recession dates, or price floors. These are selected consequential examples, not a statistical ranking of forecasting skill.

## Best takes

| Take | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| [Uranium, October 2020](articles/038.md#038-c11) | Expected supply constraints to push uranium above $50/lb by the middle of the decade. It reached $58.20 in March 2022 and $91 by December 2023. This combined a clear mechanism, a price threshold, and a deadline. Commodity appreciation does not by itself establish every uranium fund's or miner's return. |
| [Enterprise Products Partners, October 2020](articles/037.md#037-c06) | The 3-5-year patient-ownership thesis worked. The five-year vendor-adjusted return proxy was approximately +160%, versus +99% for SPY. The comparison includes the vendor's distribution and split adjustments, before fees and taxes. |
| [Patient Bitcoin accumulation, October 2022](articles/094.md#094-c23) | The 3-5-year thesis worked at the first three-year checkpoint: approximately $19,641 to $122,318. The immediate drawdown was substantial. Credit belongs to the long-horizon thesis, not precise bottom calling; the five-year endpoint remains ahead of the audit cutoff. |
| [Egyptian pound devaluation, October 2023](articles/122.md#122-c38) | Expected devaluation in Q1 2024. Exchange-rate unification occurred March 6, 2024. This was an unusually specific, successful timing call. The date alone does not prove every proposed political motive. |
| [Spring inflation, March 2021](articles/050.md#050-c07) | Expected CPI inflation above 3%, potentially above 4%. April reached 4.2%, and May 5%. The initial inflation surge was correctly anticipated; later inflation and monetary-policy expectations were weaker. |

## Worst takes

| Take | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| [Underestimating Fed tightening, January 2022](articles/076.md#076-c08) | Took the under against a market pricing roughly seven rate increases over two years. The Fed instead reached 5.25-5.50% by July 2023. Economic weakness and debt burdens did not constrain tightening as quickly as expected. |
| [2023 recession base case, December 2022](articles/100.md#100-c02) | Manufacturing and housing weakness did not become the anticipated economy-wide recession. The possible delay into 2024 did not rescue the forecast. |
| [Bank-risk diagnosis, January 2023](articles/101.md#101-c27) | Emphasized loan losses as banks' biggest risk. The ensuing acute failures exposed interest-rate losses, uninsured deposits, and rapid withdrawals. This was an explanatory miss, not merely a timing error; vulnerabilities differed across banks. |
| [Adobe, March 2021](articles/050.md#050-c26) | The five-year shareholder-compounding thesis failed: approximately -44%, versus +79% for SPY, despite continued business growth. The original stance was borderline attractive with a small initial position, not an unqualified full-position recommendation. |
| [Terra relative to Ethereum, April 2022](articles/081.md#081-c38) | Included Terra among chains she was somewhat bullish on relative to Ethereum. It collapsed about six weeks later. The same article also [correctly explained its death-spiral risk](articles/081.md#081-c23) and urged caution. The risk diagnosis and favorable relative stance deserve separate judgments. |
| [Bitcoin's November low, December 2025](articles/178.md#178-c05) | Leaned toward the November low holding over the next few months. Return comparisons test the published thesis at its stated horizon. They do not reconstruct actual portfolio weights, executions, exits, fees, or taxes. The complete reviews retain qualifications and evidence limitations.

## Most repeated points

These are recurring themes, not an exact frequency ranking. The same idea appears under multiple thesis families, and repetition is not independent confirmation.

1. **Government debt eventually changes monetary policy.** Deficits, interest costs, and refinancing needs create pressure for accommodation and financial repression. A recurring weakness is expecting that constraint to bind too soon.
2. **Follow the movement of money.** Interest rates alone are insufficient. Watch bank reserves, the Fed's balance sheet, Treasury cash balances, reverse repos, and global liquidity. These are different balance-sheet quantities, not interchangeable pools of money available for stocks.
3. **The dollar is a global funding system.** Dollar strength can squeeze borrowers outside the United States. Countries' foreign-currency debts and external balances matter enormously.
4. **Physical supply responds slowly.** Mines, energy production, grids, and infrastructure require years of investment. Low prices and underinvestment can create later shortages, subject to demand and technology changes.
5. **Bitcoin and other crypto assets are different propositions.** Bitcoin is generally treated as a scarce monetary network; most other tokens are speculative technology investments. Custody, counterparty risk, and the chosen investment vehicle remain important.
6. **Cash flow, valuation, and financing determine whether an investor can wait.** Durable businesses, manageable debt, and cash returned to owners can support patient ownership. The growth-stock misses show the difficulty of applying this principle consistently.
7. **The 2020s may favor scarce assets, value, and selected international markets.** This is a recurring investment preference, not a settled victory. The decade remains unfinished, and technology leadership has supplied substantial counterevidence.

## How to use the framework

Ask: **Who owes what, in which currency, at what interest rate? Who receives the spending? What physical supply cannot expand quickly? What price am I paying, and can I survive long enough for the thesis to work?**

Then add the question the weaker calls most need: **What proves the constraint becomes binding within my investment horizon?**
