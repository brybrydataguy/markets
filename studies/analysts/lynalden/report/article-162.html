Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

Reserve change equals Fed asset change minus TGA change minus other liability changes; a negative TGA change offsets negative assets.

Assumptions: Other liabilities fixed; Treasury cash spending and Fed runoff settle through reserves

With other balance-sheet changes fixed, Treasury spending raises reserves while Fed asset runoff reduces them. Their simultaneous occurrence can offset in the aggregate, although the actual sizes and settlement dates determine the net result.

New debt ratio equals old ratio times (1+debt growth)/(1+denominator growth), a factor below one.

Analytical tools and framework

  • GDP contribution decomposition: Separate imports, inventories and domestic demand (image not visually inspected)
  • Bank cash and securities ratios: Liquidity composition, not full leverage (image not visually inspected)
  • FDIC problem banks and losses: Distribution matters alongside aggregate totals (image not visually inspected)
  • Tangible book and FAST Graphs: Distinguish accounting value, normalized earnings and forecasts (image not visually inspected)
  • Debt/M2 and monetary base/loans: Denominator and operating-regime sensitivity (image not visually inspected)

A bank’s funding and asset duration must be analyzed together.

A monetary ratio is not a substitute for bank capital and credit analysis.
