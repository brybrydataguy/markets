200-C04 · explanation · source blocks 27, 30, 31, 33, 35

Yield minus M2 growth measures money-share dilution.

Right
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted
Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

1.05/1.057 - 1 = -0.0066225; subtraction approximates this as -0.7%.

Assumptions: Wealth comillion pounds at 5%; M2 grows 5.7%; Compare wealth with aggregate M2, not consumer prices

Under a constant nominal return and an M2 denominator growing at a fixed rate, the relative monetary share changes by (1+r)/(1+g)-1. At 5% and 5.7%, that is -0.662%, close to the stated subtraction approximation. A bond’s interim market value and reinvestment returns can differ from its quoted yield.

200-C05 · explanation · source blocks 33, 37, 38, 40, 41, 42

Money-share dilution determines purchasing-power performance.

Wrong
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted

A share of M2 and purchasing power are different quantities. With 5% nominal return, 5.7% M2 growth and 3% price inflation, monetary share falls while purchasing power rises 1.94%. Changes in real output, money demand and velocity prevent the M2 subtraction from being a general real-return measure. This distinction matters to the report’s age-based portfolio conclusions.
