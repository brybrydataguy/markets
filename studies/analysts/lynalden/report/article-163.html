163-C09 · explanation · source blocks 63

Longer issuance can pressure long yields.

Right
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted
Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

Higher supply clears at a lower bond price; fixed cash flows at that lower price imply a higher yield.

Assumptions: Downward-sloping bond demand; Other determinants unchanged; Additional duration sold to private investors

Holding demand and other conditions fixed, increasing duration supplied to private investors requires a lower price or greater willingness to hold risk. Actual yields also reflect growth, inflation and policy expectations, so the conditional mechanism is not a deterministic price forecast.

Moving principal beyond the stress interval reduces required cash outflow during that interval.

When a liability matures later and near-term cash obligations are smaller, a borrower has fewer immediate refinancing demands, all else equal. This reduces one risk channel but does not guarantee solvency or protect shareholders from dilution and NAV-premium compression.

163-C21 · explanation · source blocks 125, 126, 127, 128, 131, 133, 134, 135, 138

Safe businesses can be overpriced stocks.

Right
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted
Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

With EPS +10% and P/E -20%, price changes by 1.10*0.80-1 = -12%.

Assumptions: Price equals EPS times P/E; EPS grows less than the multiple contracts

A constant business earnings path does not fix the stock multiple. If price/earnings contracts sufficiently, price can fall despite earnings growth. This validates the valuation mechanism, without proving that a named stock must fall within an unspecified period.
