194-C04 · explanation · source blocks 24, 25, 26

Fiscal dominance weakens conventional tightening.

Mixed
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted
Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

Interest cost rises by refinanced principal times the rate increase; this accounting channel alone does not determine net aggregate demand or inflation.

Assumptions: Debt refinances at a higher rate; Principal and other fiscal choices held constant

Higher refinancing rates increase government interest costs as debt rolls over, but tightening also affects private credit, exchange rates and aggregate demand. The conclusion that fiscal deficits are the primary inflation cause requires a model separating pandemic supply disruption, fiscal transfers and energy shocks. Debt above 100% is not a universal regime threshold.

Large winners can offset smaller losses.

Right
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted
Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

A 5x terminal value produces +400%; a 50% loss produces -50%; eight equal-sized losses offset one such gain.

Assumptions: Equal initial capital in each comparison; No leverage or additional capital flows

This is arithmetic conditional on position sizes. A 5x outcome adds four units of initial capital, offsetting eight half-unit losses at equal starting weights. It does not guarantee that future winners occur, nor excuse increasing a losing position without a refreshed thesis.
