The $500 Billion AI Infrastructure Boost

Refining constraints and third-party AI funding.

Open original members article ↗ · Retrieved November 7, 2026
Right · 2Wrong · 2Mixed · 12Not testable · 2Too early · 3Evidence needed · 1
The refining bottleneck and proposed infrastructure financing are well grounded. The strongest caveats concern accounting and causal inference: a crack spread is a margin, leases usually remain on balance sheet, and faster hiring by AI adopters does not prove economy-wide job creation. Long-horizon Bitcoin and option outcomes remain open.
Coverage. Full numbered text and image inventory read. All material theses across macro, digital assets, investment analysis and concluding sections considered. Position listings and incidental descriptive statistics are excluded unless they support a thesis.

195-C02 · explanation · source blocks 11

Wrong
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted

A 3-2-1 spread is a theoretical gross refining margin: product value less crude input cost, normalized per barrel. It is not the operating cost of refining. Energy, labor, maintenance and other costs must still be deducted to estimate net profitability.

195-C13 · explanation · source blocks 65, 66, 67, 68

Leasing redistributes infrastructure investment risk.

Mixed
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted

Third-party ownership can transfer residual hardware risk and upfront financing. The article expressly acknowledges multiyear customer obligations and only partial risk transfer. Ordinary operating leases generally create right-of-use assets and lease liabilities under ASC 842, so this is not an automatic removal of balance-sheet obligations. A compute-service contract also requires separate analysis of whether it contains a lease.
