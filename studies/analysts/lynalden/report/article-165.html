165-C08 · explanation · source blocks 35, 40

Higher rates also increase fiscal interest transfers.

Right
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted
Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

Interest expense on repriced debt equals principal times rate; a higher rate raises that flow.

Assumptions: Principal unchanged; Debt reprices at a higher rate; No offsetting fiscal change

As debt refinances, a higher borrowing rate raises interest expense for unchanged principal and maturity structure. The increase is gradual and depends on issuance, inflation-linked debt and Fed remittances. It is an income channel, not a proof that the whole rate hike is stimulative.

165-C23 · explanation · source blocks 135, 136, 137, 138

Noninterest reserves would hurt bank earnings.

Right
HorizonNot specified
ConditionsNot specified
Author modalityasserted
Deductive reasoning, not an empirical outcome test

Annual income falls by added required reserves times the foregone asset yield.

Assumptions: Required reserve increase replaces interest-earning assets; Reserve rate zero; No offsetting funding repricing

If a bank must replace an interest-earning asset with an equally sized noninterest reserve balance, income falls by the foregone yield, absent offsets. The report treats this as a policy risk, not an enacted reform. Deposit repricing or other regulatory changes could change the net effect.

Analytical tools and framework

  • Fiscal versus private credit: Flow size does not identify causal dominance (image not visually inspected)
  • Fed projections: Conditional policymaker forecasts (image not visually inspected)
  • Treasury-company NAV premiums: Financing access and reflexive equity valuation (image not visually inspected)
  • HODL waves and MVRV: Coin-age and cost-basis proxies (image not visually inspected)
  • Bank Treasury ratios and SLR: Separate leverage, liquidity and risk-weighted constraints (image not visually inspected)
